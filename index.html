About

From military history to the post-Soviet world.

My path to foreign-policy history began with a family archive documenting an American airman held as a prisoner of war by Nazi Germany.

That project raised broader questions about military strategy, state responsibility, international law, and what happens to individuals when political and military institutions fail.

Russian-language study and archival research carried those questions into the post-Soviet era. My dissertation now examines how American and Russian officials tried to construct a new security order during the 1990s—and why cooperation hardened into mistrust.

Beyond the archive

Photography is another part of how I study place, memory, scale, and political space. The images throughout this site come from research travel and fieldwork.

Research

The United States, Russia, and the Post–Cold War Security Order, 1992–2000

The 1990s were not simply an interval between the Cold War and renewed rivalry. They were a contested attempt to define partnership, sovereignty, economic reform, and the rules of international security.

My dissertation reconstructs that effort through diplomacy, economic assistance, security cooperation, and competing visions of political order. It asks how American and Russian officials imagined the relationship after the Soviet collapse, where their assumptions converged, and how disagreement hardened into mistrust.

Short biography

Holly Harris is a historian of U.S.–Russia relations and American foreign policy. A Ph.D. candidate and University Ph.D. Fellow at Southern Methodist University, she studies how Washington and Moscow attempted to construct a new international security order during the 1990s—and why cooperation gave way to mistrust.
